# Case-study bank

Each topic area gets **one classic** case (well-documented, examiner-safe) plus **one recent** case (2020 onward, more engaging, exam-relevant). Pull from this list when building decks. If the topic isn't covered, generate a fresh case in the same shape using your own knowledge — but bias toward famous, fact-checkable events. Avoid making up statistics; if a number isn't well-known, say "approximately" or describe the direction only.

The "Theory link" line tells the student which mechanism the case illustrates — this is the line that goes into `link_to_theory` on the slide spec.

---

## Topic 1 — Basic ideas / scarcity / PPC

**Classic — UK rationing in WWII.** Scarcity of food and fuel during 1939–45 forced explicit non-price rationing (ration books). Illustrates the fundamental problem: choices must be made when wants exceed resources.
*Theory link:* Scarcity forces resource allocation by non-price mechanisms when the price system is suppressed.

**Recent — COVID PPE shortages, 2020.** Globally, hospitals could not procure masks/gloves at any price for several weeks. Illustrates short-run resource constraints and the cost of switching production.
*Theory link:* Movement inside the PPC (idle resources) coexisting with shortages where the curve has not yet shifted out.

## Topic 2 — Demand, supply, elasticity, equilibrium

**Classic — OPEC oil shock, 1973.** Saudi-led embargo cut crude supply; prices quadrupled. Demand was price-inelastic in the short run, so revenue rose sharply.
*Theory link:* Leftward shift in supply with low PED → large price rise, small quantity fall, higher producer revenue.

**Recent — Egg price spike, US 2022–24.** Avian flu killed ~80m laying hens; retail egg prices roughly doubled YoY.
*Theory link:* Negative supply shock + inelastic demand → sharp price rise.

## Topic 3 — Government intervention / inequality

**Classic — UK National Minimum Wage, 1999.** Set at £3.60/hr. Predicted job losses largely did not materialise in the affected sectors; LPC reports cite limited disemployment.
*Theory link:* Price floor above equilibrium wage; real-world labour-market frictions soften textbook outcome.

**Recent — UK sugar tax (Soft Drinks Industry Levy), 2018.** Reformulation eliminated ~50% of sugar in covered drinks within two years. Public Health England data.
*Theory link:* Indirect tax on a demerit good shifts S left; producers respond by reformulating rather than raising price.

## Topic 4 — Macro: AD/AS, growth, unemployment, inflation

**Classic — UK stagflation, mid-1970s.** Inflation peaked above 24% (1975) while unemployment rose — challenged the simple Phillips curve.
*Theory link:* Leftward SRAS shift (cost-push) → higher P and lower Y simultaneously.

**Recent — Post-COVID inflation surge, 2021–23.** Global CPI inflation hit 40-year highs (UK ~11% Oct 2022). Combination of supply disruption + AD bounce-back + energy shock.
*Theory link:* Combined AD right-shift and SRAS left-shift produced a textbook cost-push + demand-pull episode.

## Topic 5 — Macro policy

**Classic — Volcker disinflation, US 1979–82.** Fed funds rate raised above 19% to crush double-digit inflation; engineered recession but broke inflation expectations.
*Theory link:* Aggressive contractionary monetary policy → AD left-shift, lower π, sharp Y fall.

**Recent — UK "mini-budget" market crisis, Sep 2022.** Unfunded tax cuts spooked gilt markets; £/$ hit record low, BoE forced into emergency bond purchases.
*Theory link:* Fiscal credibility shock; expansionary unfunded policy can be undone by adverse market response.

## Topic 6 / 11 — International trade, exchange rates, BoP

**Classic — Plaza Accord, 1985.** G5 coordinated to weaken the US dollar; yen and DM appreciated sharply. Aim: correct US trade deficit.
*Theory link:* Coordinated intervention in the FX market shifts S of dollars right, lowering e.

**Recent — US–China tariffs, 2018–present.** Section 301 tariffs of 7.5–25% on c.$370bn of Chinese goods; Chinese retaliation in kind.
*Theory link:* Tariff raises domestic price, reduces import volume, creates DWL and revenue triangle; complicated by global value chains.

## Topic 7 — Microeconomics (A2): firms, market structures

**Classic — De Beers diamond cartel, 20th century.** Controlled >85% of world rough-diamond supply via the Central Selling Organisation; managed price by stockpiling.
*Theory link:* Pure monopoly with output restriction to maintain price above MC.

**Recent — Google EU antitrust fines, 2017–19.** €8.25bn cumulative over Shopping, Android, AdSense cases.
*Theory link:* Dominant firm exploiting platform position; regulator intervention to restore contestable conditions.

## Topic 8 — Government micro intervention / labour

**Classic — UK miners' strike, 1984–85.** Wage and pit-closure dispute; illustrates monopsony employer (NCB) facing organised labour.
*Theory link:* Bilateral monopoly in the labour market; outcome depends on relative bargaining power.

**Recent — Amazon warehouse unionisation, US 2022 (Staten Island JFK8).** First successful Amazon union vote.
*Theory link:* Movement from quasi-monopsony toward a more competitive wage-setting equilibrium.

## Topic 9 — Macro A2: money, banking, growth

**Classic — Quantitative Easing (UK/US), 2008–13.** Central banks bought trillions in government bonds to push down long-term rates after CB rate hit zero lower bound.
*Theory link:* Increase in MS shifts MS-curve right → lower r, asset prices rise, AD right.

**Recent — BoE QT and rate-hiking cycle, 2022–24.** BoE Bank Rate raised from 0.1% to 5.25%; balance sheet shrunk via active gilt sales.
*Theory link:* Contractionary monetary policy; MS left + interest rate up → AD left, lower π.

## Topic 10 — Macro policy synthesis

**Classic — Asian financial crisis, 1997–98.** Speculative attacks on Thai baht spread across SE Asia; IMF austerity programmes deeply contested.
*Theory link:* Fixed exchange rates + open capital accounts + weak banking → unsustainable trilemma.

**Recent — Argentina dollarisation debate, 2023–24.** Milei elected on a dollarisation platform; partial liberalisation; inflation peaked above 200% YoY.
*Theory link:* Trade-off between monetary sovereignty and credibility; high inflation as a tax on real money balances.

## Topic 11 — Development & globalisation

**Classic — China's WTO accession, 2001.** Export share of GDP rose, lifted ~800m out of extreme poverty by 2020 (World Bank).
*Theory link:* Trade liberalisation + comparative advantage drove rapid growth and structural change.

**Recent — Friend-shoring & semiconductor supply chains, 2022–present.** US CHIPS Act ($52bn), EU Chips Act (€43bn); reshoring of fab investment.
*Theory link:* Strategic deviation from free trade; trade-off between efficiency (comparative advantage) and resilience.
