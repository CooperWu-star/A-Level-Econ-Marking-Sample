# CIE A-Level Economics — Levels and Assessment Objectives

This is the working reference for marking. It is synthesised from published CIE 9708 mark schemes and is intended to capture the *pattern* of how examiners award marks, not to reproduce any single specific mark scheme verbatim.

## Assessment Objectives

CIE assesses four AOs in Economics:

### AO1 — Knowledge and understanding
The candidate demonstrates accurate, relevant economic knowledge: definitions, theories, formulas, concepts. At top band, terminology is precise (e.g. "price elasticity of demand" used correctly with awareness of its sign and what determines its magnitude).

Markers of strong AO1:
- Correct definitions of all key terms in the question
- Accurate use of formulas where relevant (e.g. PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP)
- Reference to recognised theory by name (Keynesian multiplier, comparative advantage, marginal cost pricing)

Markers of weak AO1:
- Vague language ("demand goes up a lot")
- Confused or missing definitions
- Misapplied formulas

### AO2 — Application
The candidate applies economic concepts to the specific context: the country, the firm, the market, the extract, the data given. Application is the AO most often missed by capable students who default to writing general theory.

Markers of strong AO2:
- Direct reference to data values from the extract (with units)
- Naming the country/firm/market and tailoring analysis to it
- Recognising features of the context that shape the answer (e.g. "as a developing economy with limited tax base…")

Markers of weak AO2:
- Generic essay that would work for any country
- Extract ignored or referenced only in introduction
- "Application" that is just restating the question

### AO3 — Analysis
The candidate develops chains of economic reasoning, uses diagrams correctly, and explains causal mechanisms.

Markers of strong AO3:
- Multi-step causal chains: A → B → C → D
- Diagrams that are referenced and integrated into the prose
- Clear identification of direction and magnitude of effects

Markers of weak AO3:
- Single-step assertions with no mechanism
- Diagrams unlabelled, unreferenced, or wrong
- "Demand will fall" with no explanation of why

### AO4 — Evaluation
The candidate weighs arguments, considers alternative views, and reaches a supported, conditional judgement.

Markers of strong AO4:
- Evaluation by **magnitude** (how big is the effect, given elasticities)
- Evaluation by **time horizon** (short-run vs long-run)
- Evaluation by **assumption** (what does this rely on?)
- Evaluation by **context** (does it hold for this country/firm/market?)
- Evaluation by **counter-factor** (what could offset this?)
- Conclusion that is conditional, not a fence-sit

Markers of weak AO4:
- "It depends" with no follow-through
- Listing pros and cons without weighing them
- Conclusion that contradicts the analysis
- Generic "in conclusion, both sides have merits"

## Level descriptors — 12-mark "Discuss" / "Evaluate" parts

These are the four levels CIE examiners apply. The exact mark range within a level depends on the question, but the pattern is consistent.

### Level 4 (10–12 marks) — Top band
- Accurate, well-developed analysis with clear chains of reasoning
- Diagram(s) used correctly and integrated into argument
- Strong application to the specific context throughout (not just in intro)
- Evaluation is explicit, reasoned, and uses at least two evaluation dimensions (magnitude, time, assumption, context, counter-factor)
- Conclusion is supported and conditional
- Economic terminology is precise

To hit 12/12, the answer typically needs evaluation that is *contextual* — that is, the evaluative points actually depend on features of this specific question rather than being a generic "in the long run, things might change."

### Level 3 (7–9 marks) — Good but not top
- Analysis is mostly accurate but may have one weak chain or missing step
- Diagram present but may be partly unintegrated
- Application is present but inconsistent — strong in some paragraphs, missing in others
- Some evaluation, but it may be generic ("it depends on elasticity") rather than developed
- Conclusion present but may be fence-sitting

The most common reason an answer stalls at Level 3: evaluation is *attempted* but not *developed*. The candidate says "however, this depends on time" but does not actually explain why short-run differs from long-run for this specific case.

### Level 2 (4–6 marks) — Some development
- Analysis is partial; chains of reasoning are short or have gaps
- Diagram may be missing, incorrect, or unlabelled
- Limited application — mostly general theory
- Evaluation is brief or absent — perhaps a single line at the end
- Definitions present but may be imprecise

### Level 1 (1–3 marks) — Basic
- Some relevant economic knowledge but no developed analysis
- Often a list of points rather than connected argument
- No diagram or seriously flawed diagram
- No real evaluation
- Significant terminological errors

## Level descriptors — 8-mark "Explain" parts

8-mark parts are pure analysis — evaluation is not required. Levels are typically three rather than four.

### Level 3 (6–8 marks)
- Accurate, developed explanation with clear economic reasoning
- Diagram correctly used and integrated
- Applied to the context

### Level 2 (3–5 marks)
- Partial explanation; reasoning has gaps or is shallow
- Diagram may be present but imperfectly used
- Limited application

### Level 1 (1–2 marks)
- Some relevant knowledge but minimal explanation
- Often definitional only

## Level descriptors — Data response parts

Lower-mark data-response parts (2–4 marks) tend to be marked point-by-point rather than by level:
- **(a) Identify/State (2 marks):** Two correct points from the data, each earning 1 mark.
- **(b) Calculate/Explain (4 marks):** Method + correct calculation OR explanation with reasoning. Show working.
- **(c) Analyse (6 marks):** Two-level mark scheme. L1 (1–3) for basic explanation; L2 (4–6) for developed analysis with diagram and application to the extract.

When marking data-response, always check that the candidate has *used the extract*. Answers that ignore the extract are heavily penalised on AO2.

## How to place a mark within a level

Knowing the level isn't enough — students need to know where in the level they sit. Use this:

- **Top of level:** All descriptors fully met, no significant gaps.
- **Middle of level:** Most descriptors met; one notable weakness.
- **Bottom of level:** Just into this level; on the borderline with the level below.

When awarding, explicitly say: "This is mid-Level 3 because the analysis is solid but the evaluation, while present, is generic rather than developed."
