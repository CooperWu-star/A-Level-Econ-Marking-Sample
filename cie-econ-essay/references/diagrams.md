# CIE Economics — Diagram Reference

When the skill describes a diagram in a sample essay, describe it in enough detail that an examiner could draw it correctly from the description alone: axes (with units where relevant), curves, shifts, before-and-after equilibria, and any labelled areas.

Format for diagram descriptions in essays:

```
[Diagram: <name>. Axes: <x>, <y>. Curves: <list>. Action: <shift / intersection>. Equilibrium: <before → after>. Labels: <areas, prices, quantities>.]
```

## Microeconomics

### Demand and supply
- Axes: Quantity (x), Price (y)
- Curves: D downward sloping, S upward sloping
- Common shifts: D right (income↑, tastes, substitutes), D left (income↓ for normal goods), S right (input costs↓, tech), S left (input costs↑, taxes)
- Equilibrium: P*, Q*
- Specific tax: parallel shift of S upward by the tax amount; show tax revenue (rectangle), DWL (triangle), consumer vs producer incidence

### Price elasticity of demand
- Show steep (inelastic) vs flat (elastic) D curves
- For revenue effects: compare TR rectangles before and after a price change on each
- Key labels: %ΔQd, %ΔP

### Externalities — negative production
- Axes: Q, P (or Cost/Benefit)
- Curves: MPC (= S), MSC above MPC by external cost, MPB (= D = MSB)
- Free market eq: MPC = MPB at Q₁
- Social optimum: MSC = MSB at Q*
- Welfare loss: triangle between MSC and MPB, from Q* to Q₁
- Policy: tax shifts MPC up to MSC (internalises the externality)

### Externalities — positive consumption
- MPB and MSB (with MSB above MPB by external benefit)
- MPC = MSC
- Under-consumption at free market relative to social optimum; subsidy or provision corrects

### Market structures

**Perfect competition (firm):**
- Axes: Q, P/Cost
- Curves: AR=MR=D (horizontal at P*), AC (U-shape), MC (rising, crosses AC at min)
- Short-run: can show supernormal profit (P > AC at Q where MR=MC) or loss
- Long-run: P = AC at min, normal profit only

**Monopoly:**
- Axes: Q, P/Cost
- Curves: D (downward), MR (steeper, below D), AC, MC
- Profit-maximising: MC = MR at Qm; price Pm read off D at Qm
- Supernormal profit rectangle: (Pm − AC at Qm) × Qm
- Allocative inefficiency: P > MC; DWL triangle between D and MC from Qm to allocatively efficient Q

**Oligopoly — kinked demand:**
- Axes: Q, P
- D curve kinked at current P*: elastic above (rivals don't follow price rises), inelastic below (rivals match price cuts)
- MR has vertical discontinuity at Q*
- Shows price rigidity: shifts in MC within the gap don't change P

### Labour markets
- Axes: Quantity of labour, Wage rate
- Curves: D for labour (MRP), S for labour
- Minimum wage: horizontal line above equilibrium; shows unemployment as gap between Qs and Qd at min wage
- Monopsony: MC of labour above S; firm hires where MC = MRP, pays wage from S curve at that Q

## Macroeconomics

### AD-AS
- Axes: Real GDP (Y, x-axis), Price level (PL, y-axis)
- AD: downward sloping
- SRAS: upward sloping
- LRAS: vertical at Yfe (Classical) OR horizontal then vertical (Keynesian)
- Inflationary gap: AD intersects SRAS to the right of Yfe
- Deflationary gap: AD intersects SRAS to the left of Yfe
- Fiscal/monetary expansion: AD shifts right; show new equilibrium

### Phillips curve
- Axes: Unemployment rate (x), Inflation rate (y)
- SRPC: downward sloping (trade-off)
- LRPC: vertical at NAIRU
- Show short-run movement along SRPC, then long-run shift of SRPC if expectations adjust

### Multiplier / circular flow
- Multiplier formula: 1 / (1 − MPC) or 1 / MPW
- Show on AD-AS: initial injection shifts AD by ΔG, final shift by ΔG × k

### Balance of payments / exchange rates
- Axes: Quantity of currency (x), Exchange rate (y, e.g. $ per £)
- D and S of the currency
- Appreciation: D right or S left → ER up
- Depreciation: opposite
- J-curve: time on x-axis, current account balance on y; initial worsening, eventual improvement after depreciation (Marshall-Lerner met in long run)

### Production possibility curve
- Axes: Two goods (e.g. consumer goods, capital goods)
- Concave outward (increasing opportunity cost)
- Points inside (unemployment), on (efficient), outside (unattainable currently)
- Outward shift: growth (capital accumulation, tech, labour force)

### Lorenz curve
- Axes: Cumulative % of population (x), Cumulative % of income (y)
- Line of equality: 45° line
- Lorenz curve: bowed below; further from 45° = more unequal
- Gini coefficient: area between line of equality and Lorenz curve, ÷ total area under 45° line

## Tips when describing diagrams in essays

1. **Always label axes.** Including units where relevant ($/unit, %, real GDP).
2. **Name the curves.** Don't just say "the curve shifts" — say "the SRAS curve shifts left from SRAS₁ to SRAS₂".
3. **Show before and after.** Subscript notation (P₁ → P₂, Q₁ → Q₂) makes this clear.
4. **Mark areas if they matter.** Tax revenue, consumer surplus, producer surplus, deadweight loss — only mark them if you'll reference them in the prose.
5. **Reference the diagram in the prose.** "As shown by the shift from AD₁ to AD₂, the price level rises from PL₁ to PL₂…" — diagrams that aren't referenced earn fewer marks.
